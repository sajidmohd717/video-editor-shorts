## Selection rules

Every proposed short below is built around the channel's next retention test:

1. Open on Dario or the interviewer delivering a complete, high-consequence line.
2. Establish the subject on the first frame with a short headline; never make a pronoun carry the hook.
3. Bring original narration in second and keep narration at 30% minimum, preferably 35–40%.
4. Use a new retention beat every 5–8 seconds: complication, evidence, reversal, or payoff.
5. Alternate barrage and hold. Let important claims breathe instead of cutting uniformly.
6. Use primary-source evidence and claim-shaped graphics; generic stock only bridges concrete nouns.
7. End on the strongest implication or a clean callback, with no end card.

Source windows below are editorial search ranges, not final cuts. Final clip boundaries must be cut from the 4K master, transcribed from the cut files, and checked for complete last words (F39).

## Recommended production order

Produce three pilots that test distinct hook types while keeping voice, captions, duration, and mastering stable:

1. **Why Dario left OpenAI** — conflict/personality hook.
2. **The 25% AI-risk question** — numerical stakes and interviewer rebuttal.
3. **Anthropic's unreleased “super weapon”** — capability reveal and withheld payoff.

If only one is produced first, choose **Why Dario left OpenAI**. It has a recognizable rival, a direct answer from the principal, a clear disagreement→trust reversal, and a natural human-first opening. The risk is sensationalizing “dishonesty”; every reference to it must remain explicitly attributed to Dario.

## Shared 40–45 second edit template

```text
0–4s    Source voice: strongest complete line or question, face visible
4–9s    Narration: identity + stakes in one sentence
9–16s   Source clip: first proof
16–24s  Narration + primary-source evidence or claim-shaped graphic
24–33s  Source clip: reversal/qualification
33–40s  Narration: implication, no new setup
40–45s  Source kicker or visual callback that loops into frame one
```

Transitions remain mostly hard cuts. Film burn and whoosh mark source↔narration handoffs only. One larger set-piece belongs at the pivot; it should not fire at every sentence.
